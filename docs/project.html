1. Project Overview and Scope

We want to look into the following question for this project:

Why have house prices in Turkey gone up in the last ten years?

To answer this question, we made a dataset using four monthly time-series datasets from the Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye’s (TCMB) Electronic Data Delivery System (EVDS). These datasets have:

  • Housing Price Index

  • Sales of Houses

  • Exchange Rates (USD/TRY and EUR/TRY)

  • Credit Card Sectoral Expenditures

We chose this topic because the cost of housing has become one of the most important social and economic issues in Türkiye. It is important to know what caused this rise in order to make good decisions about policy and the economy. This problem is also a good fit for a data analytics approach because it needs to combine multiple datasets, do preprocessing, and look at how things change over time and in different areas.

We want to find the main things that cause housing prices to go up. Specifically, we want to look at:

  • How exchange rates affect the price of homes

  • The link between changes in housing prices and demand (sales)

  • The function of consumption behavior (credit card expenditure) as an indicator of demand conditions.

  • Differences in how housing works in different regions

2.1 Data Source

The data used in this project were obtained from the Electronic Data Delivery System (EVDS) of the Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye (TCMB). EVDS provides official, high-frequency economic data and is widely used for academic and professional analysis.

The project is based on four datasets:

  • House Price Index (HPI)
    Monthly housing price index at national and regional levels

  • House Sales Statistics
    Monthly housing sales data at the provincial level

  • Exchange Rates (USD, EUR)
    Monthly exchange rate data

  • Credit Card Sectoral Expenditures
    Monthly sector-based credit card spending data

2.4 Preprocessing

The raw EVDS datasets were provided in Excel format and contained different structures (wide vs. long) and aggregation levels (province vs. region). We performed preprocessing in Python using pandas to standardize date formats, reshape wide datasets into long format, map categorical codes to meaningful labels, and align all datasets at a consistent monthly and regional level. In particular, house sales data were converted from province-level columns to regional aggregates compatible with the House Price Index. The final result consists of three clean analytical tables ready for further analysis.

Another key issue was that EVDS datasets use coded column names (e.g., KT2, T58, TR62) instead of descriptive labels. To make the data interpretable and consistent across sources, we created mapping dictionaries in Python to translate these codes into meaningful categories, provinces, and regions.

Here you can find our python code.

def split_year_month(df):
    df["Tarih"] = pd.to_datetime(df["Tarih"], format="%Y-%m")
    df["Year"] = df["Tarih"].dt.year

    return df

3. Analysis

This section presents the analytical part of the project. The purpose is to examine how house prices in Türkiye changed over time and how these changes are related to selected macroeconomic and demand-side indicators. The analysis focuses on housing price index movements, housing sales, exchange rates, regional differences, and consumer expenditure patterns.

The main research question is:

Why have house prices in Türkiye increased strongly in the last ten years?

To answer this question, the analysis uses three datasets:

  • Konut_Index_Sales: regional house price index and total housing sales data.
  • Conversion_Rates: monthly USD/TRY and EUR/TRY exchange rates.
  • Expense_Quantity: credit card expenditure quantity data by category.

The analysis combines descriptive statistics, time-series visualization, correlation analysis, and regression modeling. The aim is not only to produce graphs, but also to interpret the patterns in relation to the housing market and the broader Turkish economy.

This finding directly supports the main motivation of the project. The issue is not only that house prices increased, but that the speed of increase became much stronger. This type of acceleration is usually connected with broader macroeconomic pressure, including inflation, currency depreciation, construction cost increases, and investment demand.

The exchange rate trend is therefore consistent with a cost-side explanation of housing price increases. When the Turkish lira loses value, imported materials, energy, equipment, and other cost components may become more expensive. These cost increases can later be reflected in house prices.

3.4 Results

The analysis provides several important results.

First, the house price index in Türkiye increased strongly during the study period. The trend is especially steep in the later years, indicating an acceleration in housing prices rather than a stable long-term increase.

Second, exchange rates increased during the same period. The visual comparison and regression models suggest a strong positive association between exchange rates and house prices. This supports the idea that currency depreciation and macroeconomic instability are important factors behind housing price increases.

Third, housing sales do not follow the same smooth upward trend as housing prices. Sales fluctuate over time, while prices rise more consistently. This means that the increase in house prices cannot be explained only by higher transaction volume or demand.

Fourth, regional analysis shows that house price increases occurred across different regions. However, regional differences are visible, meaning that national macroeconomic conditions interact with local housing market characteristics.

Finally, the model fitting section supports the visual findings. Exchange rate variables are strongly associated with the house price index, while housing sales are less stable as an explanatory variable. The first-difference and lagged models provide additional checks and show that the relationship should be interpreted carefully because the variables have strong time trends.

Overall, the results suggest that the increase in house prices in Türkiye is a multidimensional issue. Macroeconomic instability, exchange rate movements, inflationary expectations, construction costs, and regional housing market dynamics all appear to be relevant.

4. Results and Key Takeaways

Main Findings

The results show that house prices in Türkiye increased substantially during the study period. The increase became much stronger in the later years, suggesting that housing affordability became a more serious issue over time.

Exchange rates are closely related to this pattern. Both USD/TRY and EUR/TRY increased sharply, and the house price index moved in the same general direction. This relationship is economically meaningful because exchange rates affect construction costs, imported inputs, investment expectations, and inflation behavior.

Housing sales provide a different perspective. Sales volumes are volatile and do not increase as consistently as prices. This suggests that the housing price increase was not only demand-driven. Instead, cost-side pressures and macroeconomic instability likely played important roles.

Regional results show that the price increase is not limited to a single city. However, some regions display stronger or more volatile price movements. This means that both national and local factors should be considered when analyzing the Turkish housing market.

Key Takeaways

  • House prices in Türkiye increased substantially during the study period, especially in the later years.
  • Exchange rates and house prices show a strong positive relationship, suggesting that macroeconomic instability is closely related to housing price movements.
  • Housing sales fluctuate over time and do not rise as consistently as prices, meaning that price growth is not only demand-driven.
  • Regional differences exist, so national economic pressures interact with local market conditions.
  • The overall findings suggest that inflation, exchange rate depreciation, construction costs, and investment behavior are important factors behind housing price increases.

Conclusion

This project examined why house prices in Türkiye have increased over the last ten years by combining housing price index data, housing sales data, exchange rate data, and credit card expenditure data. The analysis shows that the increase in housing prices is a multidimensional issue.

The strongest empirical pattern is the parallel increase in house prices and exchange rates. This suggests that currency depreciation and macroeconomic instability are important factors behind housing price growth. The housing sector is sensitive to exchange rates because construction inputs, energy, equipment, and investment expectations can all be affected by foreign currency movements.

At the same time, housing sales do not show a continuous upward trend similar to prices. This indicates that rising prices cannot be explained only by increasing demand. Instead, the results suggest that cost-side pressures, inflation, and investment behavior played important roles.

Regional analysis further shows that the housing price increase is a national phenomenon, but not all regions are affected in exactly the same way. Local conditions such as population growth, urbanization, migration, tourism, and supply limitations may explain why some regions experience stronger price increases than others.

Overall, the findings support the conclusion that house price increases in Türkiye are associated with both macroeconomic instability and regional housing market dynamics. Future research could improve this analysis by adding mortgage interest rates, construction cost indices, household income, population growth, and housing supply indicators.
